Theory & How-to

Quality loss: L(y) = k·(y − T)²

Two production distributions to compare

Key formulas. k = A₀ / Δ₀². Expected loss per unit for a population: L̄ = k·[σ² + (μ − T)²]. Variance and off-target bias both cost money — being "within spec" is never free.

Signal-to-noise ratios

For n observations y₁…yₙ of one design across the noise conditions:

Typeη (dB)Use when
Smaller-the-better−10·log₁₀( Σyᵢ² / n )temperature, wear, leakage, defects
Larger-the-better−10·log₁₀( Σ(1/yᵢ²) / n )life, strength, efficiency
Nominal-the-best10·log₁₀( ȳ² / s² )dimensions, pressure, voltage at a target

In every case a higher η means the design is less sensitive to noise. A 3 dB gain roughly halves the mean-square deviation.

The L9 orthogonal array

Nine runs cover 3 factors at 3 levels (27 full-factorial combinations). Each level of each factor appears 3 times and is paired equally with every level of the other factors, so the level averages are unbiased estimates of main effects. The three control factors below use columns 1, 2 and 3 of the standard L9; column 4 is left empty (it would carry a fourth factor or interactions).

Two-step optimisation (nominal-the-best)

  1. Choose levels that maximise S/N (reduce variability).
  2. Use an "adjustment factor" — one that moves the mean but barely changes S/N — to bring the mean onto target.

Prediction

η̂ at the optimum = η̄ + Σ (η̄factor at chosen level − η̄). Always run a confirmation experiment; if the confirmed η is far below the prediction, strong interactions are present and the additive model is not adequate.

How to use this tool with your own data

  1. Tab 1 — enter your target, tolerance and the cost of a unit at the tolerance limit; k is derived. Enter mean and σ for two processes to see which one costs less even if both are "in spec".
  2. Tab 2 — name three control factors and their three levels, name up to four noise conditions (e.g. cold/hot ambient × new/aged TIM), and type the measured response for each of the 36 cells. Press Analyze.
  3. Tab 3 — type two concepts' results across the same noise conditions to produce the article's Figure 1 and quantify range, σ, S/N and expected loss.

Reading the main-effects plot

A steep line = influential factor. A flat line = that factor does not affect robustness (free to set by cost or packaging). The level with the highest point is the robust choice.

Automotive validation already does this

Thermal cycling, hot/cold soak, vibration, humidity, voltage disturbance, aging and tolerance studies are all outer-array noise. Taguchi's contribution is to put them at the front of design, inside the experiment, rather than at the end as a pass/fail gate.

Robustness is not extra margin added at the end. It is engineered into the concept from the beginning.
